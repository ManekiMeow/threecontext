## Headline
1. **Multiplier-symmetric four-context KS candidates are now classified** for e ≤ 5. Here e is the index of the
   multiplier subgroup H_e = e-th powers, and the symmetry is x ↦ mx + t, m ∈ H_e, on all four bases.
   - e = 2 and e = 3: **none, for any p**. Theorem 24 is a Weil-bound proof; Corollary 26 also uses the SAT
     verdicts.
   - e = 4: **exactly 10 skeletons** (Theorem 28).
   - e = 5: **exactly 19**.
   - e = 6: **at least 111**. Single-class patterns are complete; union patterns are complete for p ≥ 283 only.
   - For every e, K4-free members exist only below an explicit bound p₀(e) (Proposition 25).
2. **Every one of these passes every necessary condition we know**: K4-free, (DEG), (C2), (C4), (TRI), ϑ = 3 at
   every ray, ϑ = 4 globally, and Proposition 18's spectrum. Those conditions are therefore far from sufficient.
3. **No realization found.** On a planted realizable control of the same density, the new L-BFGS searches succeed
   and Douglas–Rachford fails, so Thursday's DR evidence meant nothing. On the candidates, every start of every
   search stalls far from zero.
4. **Flat realizations are ruled out** for:
   - p = 97, by a counting proof (Proposition 27);
   - p = 173 (e = 4), and 4 of 10 classes at e = 5 and 45 of 76 at e = 6, by t < √h.
5. **The rank-constrained-hierarchy paper** (references/) is the right framework, but it is not practical here.
   Level 2 with PPT cannot even exclude the 16-ray Shrikhande pattern, which our local ϑ test kills. Level 3 is out
   of reach at 388+ rays.

## What this means for the paper
- Clean, provable statements: Theorem 24 (no Paley-type four-context KS set), Proposition 25 (finiteness),
  Theorem 28 (the e = 4 list), and Proposition 27 (the counting obstruction to flat realizations).
- A striking negative message: symmetric constructions produce a large zoo of skeletons that pass every known
  necessary condition, yet none is realizable numerically. Deciding even one, p = 97 being the smallest, would
  settle the question for the zoo or produce the first four-context KS set.
