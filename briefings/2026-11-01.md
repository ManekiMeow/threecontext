# Briefing — Sunday 1 November 2026

Three questions came in today:
* Is the Shrikhande verdict of the rank hierarchy an artefact of our code?
* Why did we lean towards "not realizable"?
* Can we show at least one KS-uncolourable skeleton with no quantum realization?

Details are in RESULTS.md, §3.13 (rank-hierarchy paragraph) and the new §3.14. Scripts and logs are in
`ks/realize_runs/nov1/`.

## Headline
1. **The hierarchy code has no reduction bug.**
   * `ks/rankhier.py` is Eq. (40) of the paper with SEP relaxed to PPT. The U(k) reduction of Eq. (25) is exact, and
     the constraints match term by term.
   * One numerical weakness was fixed: the normalisation was 1, so small negative margins could hide in the solver
     tolerance.
   * The paper's Fig. 3 graph (11 vertices, ϑ = 4, extracted from the figure and rechecked) behaves as the paper
     says. Level 2 finds feasible points for every k ≥ 4.01 and none at k = 4 or 4.005.
   * But the threshold lies in [4, 4.01], so the paper's exclusion of dimension 4 is a sliver at most 0.01 wide. We
     could not certify it, since Clarabel runs out of memory.
   * The real variant behaves the same: feasible at 4.01, margin −8·10⁻⁶ ("inaccurate") at 4.
   * The Shrikhande pattern is cleanly feasible at k = 4 and infeasible at 3.5. Level 2 really cannot see its
     obstruction, which is the cubic (degree-3) local ϑ test. The two graphs are similar in size, not in the degree
     of their obstruction.
2. **The lean towards "not realizable" was not justified.**
   * Douglas–Rachford says nothing: it also fails on the control.
   * The flat searches and Proposition 27 only concern flat realizations, and KS sets have uneven probabilities.
   * The general search is calibrated only on a flat product control.
   * "No covariant realization" excludes only symmetric realizations.
   * The parameter count fails for the realizable control too.
   * "The zoo passes everything, so the conditions are far from sufficient" was circular.
   * RESULTS.md is corrected in five places. **Realizability of every zoo member is open.**
3. **Three KS-uncolourable skeletons with no realization, each with an exact rational certificate**
   (`ks/unrealizable_examples.py`):

   | skeleton | d, rays | combinatorial conditions | local ϑ |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Shrikhande pattern | 4, 16 | (DEG), (C4) fail | 2 everywhere |
   | F(41, 4), best SAT model | 41, 164 | all hold | ≤ 2.7925 everywhere |
   | F(137, 4), pattern (0,3,3,0,0,2) | 137, 548 | all hold | 3 at three bases, ≤ 2.6725 at one |

   * All three are K4-free, hence KS-uncolourable, and no realization exists.
   * The p = 137 one sits in the zoo's own family and dimension. It fails only at one basis.
   * Near misses like it are common at every p from 89 to 173.
4. **The local test cannot be pushed further at p = 97.**
   * The optimal face of the local ϑ SDP leaves the moduli free: a 69-dimensional projection, everything the basis
     sums allow. So flatness cannot be forced.
   * Only 12 completeness relations close inside one neighbourhood, exactly the 12 that Proposition 27 uses.
   * A non-realizability proof for a zoo member must combine several neighbourhoods.
5. **Small d.** No Cayley skeleton over Z₁₃–Z₁₇, D₁₄ or the 11 tabulated groups of order 16 even satisfies the
   combinatorial conditions, so there is no "locally consistent, d ≤ 17" example there.

## What this means for the paper
* The honest claim: symmetric families give skeletons that pass every known necessary condition, local and global.
  Their realizability is open. Flat realizations are excluded for p = 97 by a proof.
* The gap between "KS-uncolourable graph" and "quantum realizable" is real and easy to exhibit. The certified
  examples above (especially p = 137) make the point without overclaiming.

## Next steps

2. A realizability search calibrated on a non-flat, non-product control.
3. Level 2 with the Z_p ⋊ H₄ symmetry for p = 97 on a cluster. It cannot see the local test, but might see a
